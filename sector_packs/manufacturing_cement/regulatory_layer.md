# Regulatory Layer — Cement Manufacturing (India)

## Primary regulators
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Central and State
Pollution Control Boards (CPCB/SPCB), Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), Bureau
of Indian Standards (BIS), and SEBI.

## In-force requirements with OpEx impact

### Air emissions, environmental consent and waste
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and Environment (Protection) Rules:** cement kilns, clinker coolers, coal mills and grinding units must operate within notified particulate, SO₂ and NOₓ limits. Consent conditions can require bag filters/ESPs, continuous emissions monitoring, water management and environmental statements.
- **EIA Notification, 2006:** new capacity and material expansions require environmental clearance and continuing compliance with clearance conditions.
- **CPCB/SPCB consent to establish and operate:** plant-specific conditions govern stacks, fugitive dust, mining interfaces, water, hazardous waste and monitoring. Renewal and non-compliance can affect production continuity.
- **CPCB pre- and co-processing guidance:** governs acceptance, storage, pre-processing and kiln co-processing of hazardous and other waste, including laboratory controls and emissions monitoring.
- **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 and Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016:** determine manifests, authorisations and handling controls for refuse-derived fuel and hazardous AFR streams.

### Energy and carbon
- **Energy Conservation Act, 2001 and PAT scheme:** designated cement consumers receive specific-energy-consumption targets, must complete energy audits and comply through performance or Energy Saving Certificates.
- **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, 2023, as amended:** transition toward GHG-intensity targets and compliance carbon credits can change the economics of clinker factor, fuel substitution, WHR and renewable power.
- **SEBI BRSR Core:** applicable listed entities disclose assured energy, GHG, water and waste indicators, including value-chain metrics under the applicable phased timetable.

### Product quality
- **BIS Act, 2016 and cement Quality Control Orders:** cement sold in India must conform to the applicable Indian Standard and carry the Standard Mark under a valid BIS licence. Relevant standards include IS 269 (OPC 33), IS 8112 (OPC 43), IS 12269 (OPC 53), IS 1489 (PPC) and IS 455 (PSC).
- Product formulation changes intended to lower clinker factor must remain within the applicable BIS compositional and performance envelope and pass plant/laboratory controls.

### Mining, logistics and tax
- **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act and Mineral Concession Rules:** limestone royalty, dead rent, District Mineral Foundation and National Mineral Exploration Trust obligations affect quarry cost.
- **GST law:** input-tax-credit matching, reverse charge on applicable transport, and blocked-credit rules affect fuel, packing, freight, works contracts and capital maintenance.
- Railway freight classifications, terminal policies and diesel/toll changes materially affect delivered cement cost and modal economics.
